Privacy Policy for Shrinkalu

Honestware makes small Android apps. Shrinkalu does its work on your device, and we never sell your data.

Last updated: 2 October 2026

The short version

  • Your videos stay on your device. Compressing happens on your device. We never upload, see or store your files. If you tap Share, the file goes wherever you send it.
  • We have no accounts, no servers, and no analytics or crash reporting of our own. The only things we hold about you are the emails you send us, plus what Google Play shows every developer: public reviews and overall install and crash statistics.
  • The app shows ads from Google (AdMob). Google's ads software collects some device data, listed in section 4. Showing those ads, and asking for your consent where that is required, is the only reason the app itself connects to the internet.

1. Who we are and how to reach us

Shrinkalu is made by Honestware ("we"), an independent app studio. If you have a privacy question, or want to use one of the rights in section 8, email honestware.apps@gmail.com. We read every message and aim to reply within 30 days. There are no accounts, so the only thing we can match a request to is the email address you write from.

2. Your videos

Shrinkalu reads the video you choose, makes a smaller copy of it, and saves that copy in your device's Movies folder (Movies/Shrinkalu). This all happens on your device. Your videos are never sent to us or to anyone else. If you tap Share, the file goes where you send it, and from that point the app you shared it with handles it. Your original is left alone unless you choose Replace, which deletes it after the smaller copy has been written and gives that copy the original's name, in the original's folder — the app asks you first, every time, and it deletes only that one file. It never deletes anything on its own.

Access to the videos on your device. If you allow it, Shrinkalu can list the videos on your device in its own screen, with each file's size, so you can find the big ones. Android's own dialog calls this access to "photos and videos", because that is one Android permission; the app only asks for the video part of it and never reads your photos. You can choose all videos, only some videos, or don't allow, and you can change that later in the app's Settings or in Android's settings. If you don't allow it, you can still pick videos with Android's own picker or by browsing your files, at any time, and everything else in the app works.

3. What is stored, and where

On your device. The app remembers a few things locally: your preferred preset and settings, which tips you have already seen, and the presets you have made yourself. Uninstalling the app deletes them. If the app ever crashes, it also writes a short technical note about the crash (the time, the app and Android versions, the device model and the error itself) into its own private storage. It contains nothing about your videos or their names, is never sent anywhere, and is deleted when you uninstall the app. The smaller videos you have made are ordinary files: they stay in your Movies folder until you delete them, also after an uninstall.

Backups. The app's own settings are excluded from Android's cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer, so they are not copied off your device. On a new device the app starts fresh and tips may appear again.

With us. We run no accounts, no servers, and no analytics or crash reporting of our own, so the app sends us nothing. We do receive and keep the emails you send us (section 7). Like every developer, we can also see public reviews and aggregate statistics that Google Play shows us about installs, ratings and crashes; those come from Google, not from the app.

With Google. Google's ads software inside the app collects the data listed in section 4.

4. Advertising (Google AdMob)

The app is free and is paid for by ads from Google AdMob. Google's Mobile Ads SDK, which is built into the app, automatically collects and shares with Google:

  • Your IP address, which may be used to estimate the general location of your device;
  • App interactions, such as app launches, taps and video views;
  • Diagnostic information, such as app start-up time, hang rate and energy usage;
  • Device and account identifiers, such as the Android advertising ID, the app set ID, and, if applicable, other identifiers related to accounts signed in on the device.

Google uses this for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and sends it over an encrypted (TLS) connection. Google then handles it under its own rules: How Google uses information from apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy. Google may process this data in countries other than the one you live in, under its own terms.

Apart from the ads and Google's consent form, the app does not send anything to the internet by itself. Some things you tap are handled by Google Play or by another app on your device rather than by us: the "Rate the app" link, which opens Google Play; the privacy policy link, which opens your browser; and sharing a file.

Your choices

  • Your advertising ID. On Android, open Settings › Privacy › Ads (some devices word this menu differently) and choose Reset advertising ID, which gives you a new ID so past ad activity is no longer linked to you, or Delete advertising ID, after which apps can no longer use an ID to personalise ads. Either way you will still see ads. Google's help page: Advertising ID.
  • Google's own ad settings. You can review or turn off personalised Google ads in My Ad Center. Even with personalisation off you will still see ads, chosen from things like the general location that comes from your IP address.
  • European Economic Area, United Kingdom and Switzerland. Before any personalised ads are shown, Google's consent form asks for your choice. Our legal basis for personalised advertising there is your consent; for the ordinary running of the app it is our legitimate interest in providing and paying for it. You can change your choice at any time in the app under Settings › Privacy options. If you refuse, you can keep using the whole app; you may still see ads, chosen without your personal data being used to personalise them.
  • United States. We do not sell personal information for money. Under some US state laws, letting Google's ads software collect the data above may count as "sharing" for targeted advertising. To stop ads being personalised, use the advertising-ID and My Ad Center choices above, or email us and we will point you to the right control.

5. Notifications

If you allow it, the app shows a notification while a job runs and when it finishes. These notifications are created on your device and contain no ads.

6. Children

Shrinkalu is made for adults (18+) and is not directed to children. If you are under 18, please don't use the app. We don't ask anyone for personal details, but Google's ads software collects the device data in section 4 from whoever is using the device, which is one reason for the age limit. If you think a child has written to us, email us and we will delete the message.

7. What we keep, and for how long

We keep the emails you send us for as long as we need them to answer you, and for about 12 months afterwards, and then delete them. If you want your message deleted sooner, ask us and we will do it. We hold nothing else that identifies you: no account, no profile, no server logs of our own.

The data collected by Google's ads software is held by Google, not by us, and we cannot look it up or delete it for you. Google's retention is described in the Google Privacy Policy, and the controls in section 4 are the fastest way to limit it.

8. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example Israel, the EU/EEA, the UK or California), you may have the right to ask what personal data is held about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to object to or restrict certain uses, to receive a copy, and to withdraw consent you have given. To use any of these, email honestware.apps@gmail.com. What we hold is limited to your emails to us, so we can usually answer quickly, and we will never ask you for an ID document.

For the data that Google's ads software collects, Google is the one holding it: use the choices in section 4, or Google's own privacy tools, and write to us if you can't find the right one and we'll help.

If you think we have handled your data badly, you can complain to your local data-protection authority. In Israel that is the Privacy Protection Authority; in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland it is the authority of the country you live in.

9. Changes to this policy

If we change how the app handles data, for example a new feature that uses the internet or a new ad provider, we will update this page and the "Last updated" date above, and list the change here when the update is released.
